Edu · Three.js

See how the code moves the camera.

A PerspectiveCamera is just six numbers — field of view, aspect, near, far, position, and where it looks. This tool draws all of them at once so you can watch the view frustum open up, intersect the screen, and decide what your canvas actually shows.

Edit any yellow value in the scene setup panel and the model updates live. Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom. On a phone the code panel sits below the view; on a wide screen it docks to the side.

How to read it

The blue screen sits fixed at the origin — think of it as the monitor, a stable reference you orbit around. The orange shape is the camera's frustum: its actual view volume, drawn from the camera body out through the near and far planes. At the default settings the frustum's edges pass exactly through the screen's corners, which is what "correctly framed" looks like. Editing the camera moves the camera against that fixed stage — it never moves your vantage point.

The screen carries two coordinate systems at once. In browser pixels the origin is the top-left corner, X runs right and Y runs down, ending at the renderer.setSize dimensions. In three.js units the origin is the center, X runs right and Y runs up. Reading the same corner in both systems is the whole point: it shows the conversion the renderer does on every frame.

Try pushing fov up and watch the frustum widen past the screen; pull the camera's z back and watch the frustum's reach change while the screen stays put; swap the aspect-ratio presets and watch the screen reshape. Use ⊕ frame view to recenter your vantage if the camera wanders out of sight.
